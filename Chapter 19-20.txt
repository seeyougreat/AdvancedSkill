North Carolina Supreme Court rules most challenged ballots must stay in election count - AP News

The state Supreme Court ruled Wednesday that one of its most recent cases - a lawsuit filed in the state's highest court by Republican-backed candidate Roy Cooper - was not enough to overturn the ballot.

The decision came at a time when the state's top court is revising the law that requires voters to cast their ballots at the polls within 30 days of being registered to vote.

But in the case of the state's highest court challenge, the court rejected that argument, ruling that the law is unconstitutional because it does not require voters' identification.

The state Supreme Court has been trying to rule on the issue for years. It is expected to rule on the issue in the fall.

The legal challenge filed by Cooper, a Democrat who is challenging the constitutionality of the ballot, is the first time that a high court has rejected the idea of voting in person.

The state Supreme Court will hear arguments on the case this week, and its decision will likely be appealed to the state Supreme Court.

The court heard oral arguments at a hearing in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York, which is headed by Justice John G. Roberts Jr.

The court's decision comes after a federal appeals court in San Francisco struck down several ballot measures that had been in place since 1996.
